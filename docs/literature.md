# Literature the suite builds on

Reading list for NeuroVascularSim, grouped by the part of the suite it feeds.
Each entry was checked against PubMed metadata (authors, title, journal, year,
PMID, DOI, PMCID) on 2026-09-24. Where the online and print years differ, the
entry says so. The note after each entry says what it contributes and at which
level: **detailed** (3D vascular graph) or **mesoscopic** (laminar 2D column).

The owner's own papers are in [VISION.md](VISION.md) and summarised in
[background.md](background.md). Some papers appear in more than one part
because they matter to more than one level.

Parts:

- **A. Hemodynamic and BOLD models (mesoscopic and laminar):** Friston, Buxton, Havlicek, Uludağ.
- **B. Detailed microvascular networks, flow, O₂ and BOLD from vessel geometry:** Boas, Gagnon, Pries, Secomb, Schmid, Lorthois.
- **C. Layer- and vessel-type-dependent NVC pathways, laminar cell and vessel densities, NVU models.**
- **D. Pathological and atypical responses; other modalities** (calibrated BOLD, VASO, ASL, optical, laminar fMRI).
- **E. Capillary-to-arteriole electrical signalling and pericytes:** the Nelson group and Tsoukias-lab models.

Each part ends with the papers that were expected but not found in PubMed.
Those need checking by hand before they are cited.

## Part A: Hemodynamic and BOLD signal models (mesoscopic and laminar)

All entries checked against PubMed metadata (authors, year, title, journal, PMID, DOI, PMCID if listed) on 2026-09-24. Level tags: **[meso]** = lumped or laminar (2D cortical column) level; **[detailed]** = 3D vascular graph or first-principles level.

### 1. Balloon / windkessel foundations and BOLD signal equation

- Buxton RB, Wong EC, Frank LR (1998). Dynamics of blood flow and oxygenation changes during brain activation: the balloon model. *Magn Reson Med* 39(6):855–864. [doi:10.1002/mrm.1910390602](https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.1910390602) · PMID 9621908 — The original balloon model ODEs (venous volume v, deoxyHb q, flow-driven inflow and outflow, oxygen extraction E(f)). This is the core hemodynamic state model at the **[meso]** level.
- Mandeville JB, Marota JJ, Ayata C, et al. (1999). Evidence of a cerebrovascular postarteriole windkessel with delayed compliance. *J Cereb Blood Flow Metab* 19(6):679–689. [doi:10.1097/00004647-199906000-00012](https://doi.org/10.1097/00004647-199906000-00012) · PMID 10366199 — Windkessel CBV–CBF relation with a slow venous "delayed compliance" (rat data). Gives the flow–volume exponent (α) and the viscoelastic time constant used for the post-stimulus undershoot **[meso]**.
- Obata T, Liu TT, Miller KL, et al. (2004). Discrepancies between BOLD and flow dynamics in primary and supplementary motor areas: application of the balloon model to the interpretation of BOLD transients. *NeuroImage* 21(1):144–153. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2003.08.040](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2003.08.040) · PMID 14741651 — Revised, field-dependent coefficients k1–k3 of the BOLD signal equation. Also a generalized balloon model fitted to simultaneous ASL/BOLD data **[meso]**.
- Buxton RB, Uludağ K, Dubowitz DJ, et al. (2004). Modeling the hemodynamic response to brain activation. *NeuroImage* 23 Suppl 1:S220–S233. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2004.07.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2004.07.013) · PMID 15501093 — Review that integrates the BOLD equation, the balloon model, CBF/CMRO2 neurovascular coupling and neural adaptation into one stimulus→BOLD pipeline. It is a reference spec for the **[meso]** chain.
- Buxton RB (2012). Dynamic models of BOLD contrast. *NeuroImage* 62(2):953–961. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.01.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.01.012) · PMID 22245339 · PMC3545646 — Critical retrospective on the balloon model's assumptions: parallel CBF and CMRO2 drive, and the origin of the undershoot. Useful for listing model limitations **[meso]**.
- Kong Y, Zheng Y, Johnston D, et al. (2004). A model of the dynamic relationship between blood flow and volume changes during brain activation. *J Cereb Blood Flow Metab* 24(12):1382–1392. [doi:10.1097/01.WCB.0000141500.74439.53](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.WCB.0000141500.74439.53) · PMID 15625412 — Extends the windkessel with delayed compliance to fit CBV return-to-baseline dynamics. An alternative venous compartment formulation **[meso]**.

### 2. Friston / DCM hemodynamic line

- Friston KJ, Mechelli A, Turner R, et al. (2000). Nonlinear responses in fMRI: the Balloon model, Volterra kernels, and other hemodynamics. *NeuroImage* 12(4):466–477. [doi:10.1006/nimg.2000.0630](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2000.0630) · PMID 10988040 — The canonical "hemodynamic model": a flow-inducing signal s, flow f, and a balloon (v, q) driven by synaptic activity. This is the standard NVC→BOLD ODE set for the **[meso]** level.
- Friston KJ (2002). Bayesian estimation of dynamical systems: an application to fMRI. *NeuroImage* 16(2):513–530. [doi:10.1006/nimg.2001.1044](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2001.1044) · PMID 12030834 — Priors on the hemodynamic model parameters and EM/Gauss–Newton inversion. A source of standard parameter values and priors (κ, γ, τ, α, E0) **[meso]**.
- Friston KJ, Harrison L, Penny W (2003). Dynamic causal modelling. *NeuroImage* 19(4):1273–1302. [doi:10.1016/s1053-8119(03)00202-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1053-8119(03)00202-7) · PMID 12948688 — The bilinear neuronal state equation coupled to the hemodynamic model. Defines the DCM generative-model structure used for inversion and validation **[meso]**.
- Stephan KE, Weiskopf N, Drysdale PM, et al. (2007). Comparing hemodynamic models with DCM. *NeuroImage* 38(3):387–401. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.07.040](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.07.040) · PMID 17884583 · PMC2636182 — Generalized BOLD signal equation (classical Buxton vs. revised Obata coefficients, with ε free). Bayesian model comparison favors the nonlinear revised form, which is the recommended BOLD observation equation **[meso]**.
- Marreiros AC, Kiebel SJ, Friston KJ (2008). Dynamic causal modelling for fMRI: a two-state model. *NeuroImage* 39(1):269–278. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.08.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.08.019) · PMID 17936017 — Excitatory and inhibitory populations per region with positivity constraints. The E/I neuronal front end later adopted by P-DCM **[meso]**.
- Friston KJ, Preller KH, Mathys C, et al. (2017). Dynamic causal modelling revisited. *NeuroImage* 199:730–744. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.02.045](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.02.045) · PMID 28219774 · PMC6693530 — Replaces the neuronal Taylor approximation with a canonical-microcircuit neural mass (laminar populations), and adds NVC driven by multiple populations. Directly relevant to layer-resolved neural→NVC coupling **[meso]**. (The PubMed record lists 2017 as the online date. The issue is vol. 199, 2019.)

### 3. Physiologically informed DCM (Havlicek, Uludağ et al.)

- Havlicek M, Roebroeck A, Friston K, et al. (2015). Physiologically informed dynamic causal modeling of fMRI data. *NeuroImage* 122:355–372. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.07.078](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.07.078) · PMID 26254113 — **P-DCM**: an adaptive two-state E/I neuronal model, feedforward NVC (neuronal activity→CBF), a balloon with viscoelastic CBF–CBV uncoupling, and a field-strength-dependent BOLD equation. It is the primary **[meso]** single-compartment model to implement.
- Havlicek M, Roebroeck A, Friston KJ, et al. (2017). On the importance of modeling fMRI transients when estimating effective connectivity: A dynamic causal modeling study using ASL data. *NeuroImage* 155:217–233. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.03.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.03.017) · PMID 28323165 — Shows that transients (adaptation, post-stimulus undershoot) change connectivity estimates. Adds a joint BOLD+CBF (ASL) observation model, which is useful for multi-output validation **[meso]**. (This is the "Havlicek 2017" transients paper.)
- Havlicek M, Ivanov D, Poser BA, et al. (2017). Echo-time dependence of the BOLD response transients – A window into brain functional physiology. *NeuroImage* 159:355–370. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.07.034](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.07.034) · PMID 28729160 — Nonlinear two-compartment (intra/extravascular) BOLD signal model with TE dependence plus the dynamic balloon. Argues that CBF–venous CBV uncoupling, not CBF–CMRO2 uncoupling, explains the transients **[meso]**.
- Havlicek M, Ivanov D, Roebroeck A, et al. (2017). Determining excitatory and inhibitory neuronal activity from multimodal fMRI data using a generative hemodynamic model. *Front Neurosci* 11:616. [doi:10.3389/fnins.2017.00616](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2017.00616) · PMID 29249925 · PMC5715391 — Fits P-DCM jointly to CBF, CBV, BOLD and neuronal data (human, cat and monkey datasets). A worked example of multi-modal ground-truth-style validation **[meso]**.

### 4. Laminar (cortical-depth) BOLD models

- Heinzle J, Koopmans PJ, den Ouden HEM, et al. (2016). A hemodynamic model for layered BOLD signals. *NeuroImage* 125:556–570. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.10.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.10.025) · PMID 26484827 — Extends the DCM hemodynamic model with a per-layer balloon and inter-layer coupling for venous draining toward the surface. The first layered DCM-style **[meso]** laminar model. (Published online 2015. The issue year is 2016.)
- Markuerkiaga I, Barth M, Norris DG (2016). A cortical vascular model for examining the specificity of the laminar BOLD signal. *NeuroImage* 132:491–498. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.02.073](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.02.073) · PMID 26952195 — Steady-state cortical vascular model: laminar microvasculature plus intracortical ascending veins, built on the Uludağ 2009 signal model. Gives GE vs. SE laminar point-spread functions **[meso]**.
- Havlicek M, Uludağ K (2020). A dynamical model of the laminar BOLD response. *NeuroImage* 204:116209. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116209](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116209) · PMID 31546051 — **Laminar BOLD model (LBR)**: per-depth microvasculature balloons coupled by mass conservation to ascending and pial veins, with a depth-dependent baseline CBV. It is the reference dynamic model for the **[meso]** 2D laminar column. (The PubMed record shows 2019 as the online date. The issue is vol. 204, 2020.)
- Uludağ K, Havlicek M (2021). Determining laminar neuronal activity from BOLD fMRI using a generative model. *Prog Neurobiol* 207:102055. [doi:10.1016/j.pneurobio.2021.102055](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pneurobio.2021.102055) · PMID 33930519 — Full generative model: laminar E/I neurons → LBR → finite-voxel sampling, including angular dependence. Shows Bayesian inversion recovering depth-resolved neuronal activity, which is a template for synthetic ground-truth tests **[meso]**.
- Uludağ K (2023). Physiological modeling of the BOLD signal and implications for effective connectivity: A primer. *NeuroImage* 277:120249. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2023.120249](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2023.120249) · PMID 37356779 — Consolidated primer on P-DCM plus the laminar extension, covering the physiological origin of the overshoot and undershoot. A good design reference for the **[meso]** pipeline.
- Marquardt I, Schneider M, Gulban OF, et al. (2018). Cortical depth profiles of luminance contrast responses in human V1 and V2 using 7 T fMRI. *Hum Brain Mapp* 39(7):2812–2827. [doi:10.1002/hbm.24042](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.24042) · PMID 29575494 · PMC6866457 — Spatial deconvolution model for draining-vein leakage in depth profiles. A simple steady-state inverse of the laminar forward model **[meso]**.
- Akbari A, Bollmann S, Ali TS, et al. (2023). Modelling the depth-dependent VASO and BOLD responses in human primary visual cortex. *Hum Brain Mapp* 44(2):710–726. [doi:10.1002/hbm.26094](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.26094) · PMID 36189837 · PMC9842911 — Extends the Markuerkiaga cortical vascular model to predict laminar VASO (CBV) as well as BOLD, and fits compartment CBV changes. Useful for a CBV observation model **[meso]**. (PubMed lists 2022 as the online date.)
- Goense J, Bohraus Y, Logothetis NK (2016). fMRI at high spatial resolution: implications for BOLD-models. *Front Comput Neurosci* 10:66. [doi:10.3389/fncom.2016.00066](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2016.00066) · PMID 27445782 · PMC4923185 — Reviews laminar differences in CBF, CBV and NVC that mesoscopic BOLD models must include (for example, the negative BOLD response). Informs parameter heterogeneity across depth **[meso]**.

### 5. Uludağ group: vascular / MR-signal models (GE vs. SE, field strength)

- Uludağ K, Müller-Bierl B, Uğurbil K (2009). An integrative model for neuronal activity-induced signal changes for gradient and spin echo functional imaging. *NeuroImage* 48(1):150–165. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2009.05.051](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2009.05.051) · PMID 19481163 — Per-vessel-class (arteries → veins) intra- and extravascular GE/SE signal model up to 16.4 T, with Monte Carlo derived relaxation rates. The BOLD observation model bridging the **[detailed]** and **[meso]** levels.
- Uludağ K, Blinder P (2018). Linking brain vascular physiology to hemodynamic response in ultra-high field MRI. *NeuroImage* 168:279–295. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.02.063](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.02.063) · PMID 28254456 — Review with simulations covering penetrating-vessel spacing vs. columns, baseline CBV across layers, where CBV changes occur, and vascular latency confounds. Gives both **[detailed]** and **[meso]** design constraints. (The PubMed record shows 2017 as the online date.)
- Huber L, Uludağ K, Möller HE (2019). Non-BOLD contrast for laminar fMRI in humans: CBF, CBV, and CMRO2. *NeuroImage* 197:742–760. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.07.041](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.07.041) · PMID 28736310 · PMC12906290 — Review of laminar ASL, VASO and calibrated fMRI. Defines the non-BOLD laminar observables a **[meso]** model could also predict. (PubMed online date 2017. The issue is vol. 197, 2019.)
- Kashyap S, Ivanov D, Havlicek M, et al. (2021). Sub-millimetre resolution laminar fMRI using Arterial Spin Labelling in humans at 7 T. *PLoS One* 16(4):e0250504. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0250504](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0250504) · PMID 33901230 · PMC8075193 — Laminar CBF vs. BOLD profiles reconciled through a model-based draining-vein "convolution". An empirical check for the **[meso]** laminar model.

### 6. Spatiotemporal / neural-field hemodynamics (Robinson–Aquino)

- Drysdale PM, Huber JP, Robinson PA, et al. (2010). Spatiotemporal BOLD dynamics from a poroelastic hemodynamic model. *J Theor Biol* 265(4):524–534. [doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2010.05.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2010.05.026) · PMID 20665966 — Treats cortex as a poroelastic medium (mass and momentum conservation). Reduces to the balloon model in the lumped limit, and gives PDEs for **[meso]** tangential spatial spread.
- Aquino KM, Schira MM, Robinson PA, et al. (2012). Hemodynamic traveling waves in human visual cortex. *PLoS Comput Biol* 8(3):e1002435. [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002435](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002435) · PMID 22457612 · PMC3310706 — Predicts and confirms damped BOLD waves (2–12 mm/s) along the cortical sheet. A lateral-coupling term for the **[meso]** 2D sheet.
- Aquino KM, Robinson PA, Drysdale PM (2014). Spatiotemporal hemodynamic response functions derived from physiology. *J Theor Biol* 347:118–136. [doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2013.12.027](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2013.12.027) · PMID 24398024 — Derives the nonlinear hemodynamic wave equation and the spatiotemporal HRF (wave speed from stiffness, damping from viscosity) **[meso]**.
- Aquino KM, Robinson PA, Schira MM, et al. (2014). Deconvolution of neural dynamics from fMRI data using a spatiotemporal hemodynamic response function. *NeuroImage* 94:203–215. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.03.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.03.001) · PMID 24632091 — Uses the stHRF for joint space–time deconvolution, with synthetic ground-truth tests. A validation-methodology template **[meso]**.

### 7. Oxygen transport and multi-compartment models (Sheffield / Zheng–Mayhew)

- Zheng Y, Martindale J, Johnston D, et al. (2002). A model of the hemodynamic response and oxygen delivery to brain. *NeuroImage* 16(3 Pt 1):617–637. [doi:10.1006/nimg.2002.1078](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2002.1078) · PMID 12169248 — Replaces Buxton–Frank oxygen extraction with a dynamic capillary oxygen-transport model that has a tissue O2 state. Allows CMRO2 time series and hypercapnia **[meso]**.
- Zheng Y, Johnston D, Berwick J, et al. (2005). A three-compartment model of the hemodynamic response and oxygen delivery to brain. *NeuroImage* 28(4):925–939. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2005.06.042](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2005.06.042) · PMID 16061400 — Arterial, capillary and venous compartments with pre-capillary O2 diffusion and time-varying tissue O2. A natural intermediate between the lumped balloon and the vascular graph **[meso]**.

### 8. Riera: state-space and electrovascular models

- Riera JJ, Watanabe J, Kazuki I, et al. (2004). A state-space model of the hemodynamic approach: nonlinear filtering of BOLD signals. *NeuroImage* 21(2):547–567. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2003.09.052](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2003.09.052) · PMID 14980557 — Stochastic (physiological-noise) version of the Friston hemodynamic model with local-linearization filtering. Relevant for noise-driven simulation and state estimation **[meso]**.
- Riera JJ, Wan X, Jimenez JC, et al. (2006). Nonlinear local electrovascular coupling. I: A theoretical model. *Hum Brain Mapp* 27(11):896–914. [doi:10.1002/hbm.20230](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.20230) · PMID 16729288 · PMC6871312 — Couples a neural mass model (three subpopulations, producing LFP/EEG) to an extended balloon through an electrovascular coupling term. A joint EEG+BOLD **[meso]** forward model.
- Riera JJ, Jimenez JC, Wan X, et al. (2007). Nonlinear local electrovascular coupling. II: From data to neuronal masses. *Hum Brain Mapp* 28(4):335–354. [doi:10.1002/hbm.20278](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.20278) · PMID 16933303 · PMC6871399 — Inversion of the LEVC model from concurrent EEG and fMRI **[meso]**.

### 9. Bridging to the detailed (3D vascular-graph) level

- Boas DA, Jones SR, Devor A, et al. (2008). A vascular anatomical network model of the spatio-temporal response to brain activation. *NeuroImage* 40(3):1116–1129. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.12.061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.12.061) · PMID 18289880 · PMC2577617 — VAN model: a network of compliant vessel segments with flow, volume and O2 saturation. The prototype for the **[detailed]** graph-level hemodynamics.
- Gagnon L, Sakadžić S, Lesage F, et al. (2015). Quantifying the microvascular origin of BOLD-fMRI from first principles with two-photon microscopy and an oxygen-sensitive nanoprobe. *J Neurosci* 35(8):3663–3675. [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3555-14.2015](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3555-14.2015) · PMID 25716864 · PMC4339366 — Computes BOLD from measured 3D angiograms plus PO2 using Monte Carlo spin diffusion. The **[detailed]** reference for graph→BOLD signal synthesis.
- Báez-Yáñez MG, Siero JCW, Petridou N (2023). A mechanistic computational framework to investigate the hemodynamic fingerprint of the blood oxygenation level-dependent signal. *NMR Biomed* 36(12):e5026. [doi:10.1002/nbm.5026](https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.5026) · PMID 37643645 — Synthetic 3D vasculature (Voronoi microvessels plus macrovessels) → CBF/CBV/SO2 → susceptibility field → dynamic BOLD **[detailed]**.
- Báez-Yáñez MG, Siero JCW, Curcic V, et al. (2025). Impact of vascular architecture, oxygen saturation, and hematocrit on human cortical depth-dependent GE- and SE-BOLD fMRI signals: A simulation approach using realistic 3D vascular networks. *Imaging Neurosci (Camb)* 3. [doi:10.1162/imag_a_00573](https://doi.org/10.1162/imag_a_00573) · PMID 40800802 · PMC12319794 — Laminar GE/SE BOLD from realistic 3D networks at 7T. Directly links **[detailed]** graphs to **[meso]** depth profiles.
- Báez-Yáñez MG, Siero JCW, van Osch MJP, et al. (2026). A synthetic 3D human cerebrovascular model informed by histology for simulating the cortical depth-dependent BOLD fMRI signal. *PLoS Comput Biol* 22(8):e1014576. [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014576](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014576) · PMID 42546087 · PMC13450845 — 3D VAMOS: generates human-specific synthetic cortical vascular graphs from histology statistics and simulates laminar BOLD. A candidate generator for **[detailed]** synthetic test networks.

## Part B: Detailed microvascular networks, flow, O2 transport, and BOLD from vessel geometry

All entries were checked against PubMed (metadata via PubMed E-utilities) on 2026-09-24. Years are PubMed publication dates. Where the epub year and the print year differ, both are noted.

### 1. Vascular Anatomical Network (VAN) models and first-principles BOLD (Boas / Gagnon / Sakadžić)

- Boas DA, Jones SR, Devor A, et al. (2008). A vascular anatomical network model of the spatio-temporal response to brain activation. *NeuroImage*. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.12.061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.12.061) · PMID 18289880 · PMC2577617 — The original VAN model: a compliant vessel-graph network, with arteriolar dilation driving flow, volume and oxygenation dynamics. It is the reference design for a detailed (node/edge) hemodynamic layer.
- Fang Q, Sakadžić S, Ruvinskaya L, et al. (2008). Oxygen advection and diffusion in a three-dimensional vascular anatomical network. *Optics Express*. [doi:10.1364/oe.16.17530](https://doi.org/10.1364/oe.16.17530) · PMID 18958033 · PMC2584207 — A hybrid solver that couples intravascular O2 advection on the graph with tissue O2 diffusion on a 3D grid, time-resolved. It is the core method for detailed-level O2 transport.
- Sakadžić S, Roussakis E, Yaseen MA, et al. (2010). Two-photon high-resolution measurement of partial pressure of oxygen in cerebral vasculature and tissue. *Nature Methods*. [doi:10.1038/nmeth.1490](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.1490) · PMID 20693997 · PMC2932799 — The two-photon phosphorescence pO2 method (PtP-C343 probe) that supplies intravascular and tissue pO2 ground truth for validating O2 models.
- Sakadžić S, Mandeville ET, Gagnon L, et al. (2014). Large arteriolar component of oxygen delivery implies a safe margin of oxygen supply to cerebral tissue. *Nature Communications*. [doi:10.1038/ncomms6734](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms6734) · PMID 25483924 · PMC4260810 — Two-photon pO2 data across vessel branch orders showing substantial O2 extraction from arterioles. Useful as per-vessel-class SO2 targets for detailed simulations.
- Gagnon L, Sakadžić S, Lesage F, et al. (2015). Quantifying the microvascular origin of BOLD-fMRI from first principles with two-photon microscopy and an oxygen-sensitive nanoprobe. *J Neurosci*. [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3555-14.2015](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3555-14.2015) · PMID 25716864 · PMC4339366 — The flagship pipeline: measured angiograms and pO2 go through graph-level SO2 and Monte Carlo proton diffusion in the perturbed field to give BOLD. It breaks the signal down by vessel class, orientation and field strength. This is the detailed-level BOLD template.
- Gagnon L, Sakadžić S, Lesage F, et al. (2015). Multimodal reconstruction of microvascular-flow distributions using combined two-photon microscopy and Doppler optical coherence tomography. *Neurophotonics*. [doi:10.1117/1.NPh.2.1.015008](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.NPh.2.1.015008) · PMID 26157987 · PMC4478873 — Constrains network flow solutions on real angiograms with Doppler-OCT flows measured in individual segments. It addresses boundary conditions for graph flow solvers.
- Gagnon L, Sakadžić S, Lesage F, et al. (2016). Validation and optimization of hypercapnic-calibrated fMRI from oxygen-sensitive two-photon microscopy. *Philos Trans R Soc Lond B Biol Sci*. [doi:10.1098/rstb.2015.0359](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2015.0359) · PMID 27574311 · PMC5003859 — Uses the first-principles BOLD simulations to test the Davis/Hoge calibrated-fMRI model (α, β) and the rCMRO2 estimates. It links detailed simulations to the mesoscopic or biophysical BOLD model parameters.

### 1. Capillary K+ sensing, Kir2.1 and retrograde hyperpolarization

- Longden TA, Dabertrand F, Koide M, et al. (2017). Capillary K+-sensing initiates retrograde hyperpolarization to increase local cerebral blood flow. *Nat Neurosci*. ### Mechanism summary for modelling

1. **Sensing:** neuronal/astrocytic K+ release raises [K+]o around capillaries to ~8–10 mM. This activates capillary-EC Kir2.1, whose conductance rises with [K+]o. [PMID 28319610, 25641345]
2. **Amplification and threshold:** Kir2.1 negative-slope conductance makes the hyperpolarization regenerative. Enough capillary segments must be stimulated, and EC background conductance must be low enough, for the signal to become self-sustaining and spread instead of decaying. [PMID 32601236, 29117630]
3. **Conduction:** the hyperpolarization travels upstream (retrogradely) through homocellular EC gap junctions, fast over hundreds of µm, through the PATZ into the PA and on to pial arteries. Damaging the endothelium blocks it. [PMID 28319610, 24926076, 21199397, 22890708, 19855062]
4. **Effector:** at the PA, and at PATZ ensheathing pericytes, the EC hyperpolarization passes through myoendothelial (EC–mural) coupling into SMCs/pericytes. It closes VDCCs, lowers Ca2+ and relaxes the vessel. The largest resistance change is in the PA and PATZ. [PMID 19905969, 36802432, 36751860, 33051294, 29937277]
5. **Gating by PIP2:** Kir2.1 needs PIP2. Gq-GPCR activity (and disease) depletes PIP2, which switches Kir2.1 off and at the same time disinhibits TRPV4 Ca2+ influx. Arf6 regenerates PIP2. This is a reciprocal electrical-vs-Ca2+ switch in the EC. [PMID 29581272, 30084828, 32764146, 42412927]
6. **Ca2+ channel (slow, local):** capillary ECs produce IP3R-mediated Ca2+ events that compound with activity and cause local NO-dependent dilation. Fast electrical signals can also trigger distant Ca2+ events (electrocalcium coupling). TRPA1 starts slow Ca2+ waves in capillaries that become electrical signals in the PATZ. [PMID 34290098, 39661063, 33635784]
7. **Mechanosensing feedback:** capillary-EC Piezo1 senses flow/pressure and depolarizes/brings in Ca2+, limiting hyperemia. This is negative feedback from the flow solver to EC voltage. [PMID 35382561, 39375369]
8. **Pericyte roles by segment:** PATZ ensheathing pericytes (α-SMA+) are myogenic and fast, and they set the flow split at junctions. Thin-strand pericytes contract slowly (s–min), set basal capillary tone, and act as electrical initiators through KATP. Mural cell identity follows branch order from the PA. [PMID 33051294, 36802432, 33603231, 28933255, 38444160, 26119027, 24670647]
9. **KATP / metabolic arm:** low glucose/ATP, and adenosine (A2A/cAMP/PKA), open KATP in thin-strand pericytes and capillary ECs. The hyperpolarization enters the same EC conduction pathway and dilates upstream arterioles. [PMID 36577387, 35349300, 39630860, 38197953]
10. **Failure modes:** reduced capillary Kir2.1/PIP2 (CADASIL, AD, hypertension, TBI), impaired EC Ca2+ (tau), PA SMC Kir loss (stress), PA SMC Kv1 dysregulation (CADASIL/SAH) and pericyte KATP hyperactivity (CADASIL) each weaken or redistribute functional hyperemia. Each maps to a single parameter change on the graph. [PMID 33875602, 33763649, 34351870, 33050826, 38420777, 24808139, 29247493, 42547848]
